You pick your building on the map and answer illustrated questions you can answer from the street; the app combines the ministry’s official rapid assessment method (RYTEİE 2019 Annex A) with the site’s seismic hazard and returns a “professional inspection priority” with item-by-item reasons and an A4 report. There is no server: inputs never leave the browser.

The problem

The way to learn a building’s earthquake risk for certain in Türkiye is a risky-building assessment under Law 6306: the owner applies and pays. Before taking that step most people ask “is my building a priority?”, and all they have is hearsay or scoring tools that do not explain their method. The ministry’s own regulation contains an official rapid screening method done from the street; it is not usable by an ordinary person as written.

What it does

  • Finding the building: fully local neighbourhood search (Turkish case folding, fuzzy matching); tap a building footprint on the map, or drop a point if there is none. 2.2 million buildings in four provinces.
  • Illustrated form: 13 steps for reinforced concrete, 20 for masonry. Questions such as soft storey, short column and heavy overhang come with SVGs redrawn from the regulation’s figures. Every question has “I don’t know”: the score opens to both ends and the result becomes a range.
  • Seismic hazard: from a precomputed, versioned ESHM20 grid. Its difference from AFAD’s official value was measured at 10 points per province and folded into the range; entering the official value from e-Devlet collapses it to one number. If the uncertainty changes the priority, the result card asks for the official value.
  • Priority, not a verdict: three bands, an item-by-item contribution table, source tables and a note on the method’s accuracy. An A4 PDF report, and a “Professional inspection” page on the province’s directorate and the Law 6306 process, containing only information verified at the primary source.
  • Privacy and field use: no server, answers stay in the browser; a share link carries the result in the URL’s # fragment. After the first visit it works offline, so the form can be filled in front of the building on a weak signal.

Measurements

The struck-through value is what was found, the one beside it what was measured after the fix. Every number comes from the project’s own record.

How it was built with AI

  1. 1Four spikes first. The official text (tables read from the Official Gazette page images), hazard data (ESHM20 against 10 AFAD reports), accuracy (2023 earthquake, ACI 133 data) and address/footprints. The first research pass had said “there is no official scored method”; the primary source showed otherwise, and the product was built on that method.
  2. 2Tables bound to tests. Score tables live in JSON; expected values in the tests were written independently, and errors deliberately injected into the tables make the tests fail. Against the Python reference, 154 buildings × 3 hazard assumptions = 462 comparisons match exactly.
  3. 3Reproducible data. One command builds a province package; two runs give identical sha256 for every file. No province name is hard-coded: a new province is a new data package.
  4. 4Decisions on record. Decision log K-1…K-18: how “I don’t know” is scored, band limits and the per-province hazard range are written down with their reasons.

Takeaway

The validation data shrank the product’s claim: on 2023 data the official method separates heavy damage only as well as storey count does. Instead of hiding that, I wrote it on the Method page and moved the claim from “a more accurate prediction” to “an open, reproducible implementation of the official method with its limits written down”. The same data put “low priority does not mean safe” on the result card.

Honest limits

What this project does not do, or has not proven yet.

  • The result is a priority band, not a verdict on the building; the regulation says the method cannot be used to assess a single building’s risk.
  • Visible quality, heavy overhang, irregularities, adjacency and slope were absent from the validation data; their accuracy could not be measured.
  • Four provinces (Manisa, İzmir, Aydın, Denizli); steel, prefabricated and 8+ storey concrete buildings are out of scope.
  • No real-user testing yet.
