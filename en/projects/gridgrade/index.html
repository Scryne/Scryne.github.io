GridGrade

US electricity prices and utility report cards

Builds a price page for every US state, utility and county from the Energy Information Administration’s open data, and grades each utility A–F on price and outage minutes. The site rebuilds itself every day.

The problem

What a US household pays for electricity varies widely by state, utility and county. The data is public but scattered across spreadsheets: the answer to “is my utility expensive, and does it have many outages” does not sit on one page.

What it does

  • A keyless data pipeline: EIA’s monthly and annual files are downloaded directly; the state, utility and county model is built from them.
  • Report card: each utility is scored against all US utilities on residential price and outage minutes (excluding major event days); the method is spelled out on the site.
  • A page for every place: a ten-year price curve and US comparison on the state page, the report card and price history on the utility page, the utilities serving that county on the county page.
  • Bill calculator: computes a monthly bill from state and usage; appliance-level usage can be added.
  • Updates itself: GitHub Actions regenerates the site every day; it publishes nothing when nothing changed, and notifies search engines when something did.

Audit

In the November 2026 audit the site was walked across 14 page types at four screen widths. Three things the tests did not see came up: chart labels shrank to about 6 pixels on phones (a separate narrow SVG layout was added), 213 utilities’ reliability data was shown as “did not report”, and a calculator button wrapped onto two lines. The calculator’s result was checked by hand: $163.30 for 1,000 kWh in Alabama.

Limits

  • The data follows EIA’s release calendar; the monthly file lags by a few months and the annual utility data is for the previous year.
  • No dark theme; a deliberate design decision.
  • Most pages have not yet been offered to search engines; indexing is staged.
  • Not affiliated with EIA or any utility; grades are computed from public data only.
