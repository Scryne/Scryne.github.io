MythAtlas

An open atlas linking myths, deities and sacred sites on a map

Connects 157 myths, 212 deities and 104 sacred sites through a world map, detail pages and comparative parallels, guarded by a content-accuracy gate that keeps every image, source and UNESCO claim verifiable.

The problem

Comparative mythology data is scattered, and building an atlas that “looks good” is easy: an image, a few academic sources and a “contested” badge per record are a few lines of code. The hard part is making them true. MythAtlas’s first version fell right into that trap.

Measurements

Struck through: what was found. Beside it: measured after the fix.

September 2026 audit

The first version passed CI, but its content collapsed under measurement: 10 archaeological sources were fabricated, the same 11 theory books were attributed as boilerplate to nearly all 157 myths, 25 links were 404, the Taj Mahal was the image for 20 Hindu deities, the Pyramid of Khufu stood in for Abu Simbel, 28 sites had a fake “active excavation” badge, and parallel-myth “similarity scores” were unrelated to thematic overlap.

  • Verification pipeline: strict Wikipedia/Wikidata matching (name + type + context), UNESCO status via Wikidata SPARQL.
  • Idempotent cleanup: boilerplate citations and dead links removed; unsourced fields removed from the UI.
  • Honest metric: parallel similarity is now the Jaccard ratio of two myths’ motif sets, and the UI says so.
  • Gate: qa:content passes the clean data and finds 5,483 violations in the old; mandatory in CI.
  • Upgrade: Next 14 → 16, React 19, MapLibre 6; 0 advisories in production dependencies.

Takeaway

“Tests pass” did not measure content accuracy. Accuracy needs its own gate: a source per claim, verification per source, and a check proven to catch the old data.

Honest limits

  • No hosted deployment; the site builds and runs locally.
  • Records whose image could not be verified (36 deities, 14 sites) deliberately have none.
